## Cold open (0:00–0:30) [BG04 barricaded flat at night, then BG03 street]
*Black. KNOCK. Vini frozen behind the sofa. A shadow under the door.*
It's three in the morning. Something is knocking on Vini's front door. And if he makes a single sound, he's dead.
*KNOCK. Snap cut to daylight, the street, eighteen days earlier.*
Eighteen days ago, Vini had never survived anything harder than a Monday. Then the dead got up and started walking through his city.
They can't see very well. But they can hear everything.
So can Vini stay quiet for thirty days? He nearly does.

## Day 1: Lock the door (0:30–1:45) [BG01 flat]
Day one. Vini wakes up to forty-seven missed calls and his phone screaming an emergency alert. Outside, someone is shouting. Then they stop shouting.
His first instinct is to run outside and see what's going on. That's what most people do. And that's why most people don't make it past day one.
Rule one: on day one, stay inside. Lock the door, close the curtains, and stay away from the windows. In the first hours of any disaster, the streets are the most dangerous place to be. Crashed cars, panicked crowds, and in this case, crowds that bite.
Rule two: fill everything with water. The bath, the sink, every pan and bottle he owns. When a city breaks down, the taps are often one of the first things to go. A person needs at least two litres of drinking water a day, and a full bath holds well over a hundred litres. That's weeks of water, sitting right there in the bathroom.
*Tracker: WATER full. FOOD 3 DAYS. BATTERIES 0.*
By midnight, the shouting outside has turned into something else. A low groan. Hundreds of them.

## Day 3: The supply run (1:45–3:25) [BG03 street, BG02 supermarket]
Day three. Vini's food is running out. He's eaten a family bag of crisps, half a jar of mayonnaise, and something from the back of the freezer he can't identify. He has to go out.
Rule three: move at the quietest time, and plan your route before you leave. The supermarket is four streets away. Four streets has never felt so far.
He creeps past abandoned cars, doors still open where people jumped out and ran. One car alarm, one dropped tin, and everything on this street will know exactly where he is.
Inside, most people grabbed the wrong things. Bread, fresh meat, milk. All of it goes off within days.
Rule four: grab food that lasts. Tins, rice, pasta, peanut butter, anything that doesn't need a fridge. And grab the things nobody thinks of: batteries, a torch, a first aid kit, and a battery radio.
Vini finds a trolley with tins still in it. Beans. Actual beans. He holds one up like a trophy.
*CLANG.*
That was mistake number one. His elbow caught the shelf. One tin hits the floor, and the sound rolls down every aisle in the shop.
Vini doesn't move. At the far end of the aisle, something starts walking towards the noise.
*Tracker: FOOD 3 WEEKS. BATTERIES 8.*
He gets out through the back with the bag, the radio, and a new rule of his own: hold things with two hands.

## Day 7: The fortress (3:25–4:50) [BG04 barricaded flat, BG05 stairwell]
Day seven. Vini turns his flat into a fortress. He boards up the windows. He pushes the bookcase against the front door, then the sofa, then the fridge. Nothing is getting in.
Then he steps back, looks at it, and realises mistake number two. Nothing is getting out, either.
Rule five: never block your only way out. If they break in through a window, a sealed-up flat isn't a fortress. It's a trap. Vini spends the afternoon moving the fridge back, and makes sure he can still reach the stairwell, and from there, the roof.
Rule six: height is safety. Vini lives on the fifth floor, and the infected are terrible at stairs. He goes down to block the ground-floor door with furniture.
He's halfway through when he hears it. A slow, wet footstep. Then another. One of them is already inside, two floors down, working out how stairs go.
Vini wedges the last chair under the handle without breathing. It takes the thing eleven minutes to give up on the first step.
For the first time in a week, Vini sleeps. Badly.

## Day 12: The taps run dry (4:50–5:55) [BG06 rooftop]
Day twelve. The taps cough, splutter, and stop. The bath water is getting low.
Rule seven: when the taps stop, look up. Rain is the easiest clean water source in a British city, and Britain is not short of rain. Vini drags a tarp up to the roof and ties it between four poles, so the rain runs into buckets underneath.
He's carrying the last bucket when it slips. It bounces off the edge of the roof, and falls five floors.
*Silence. Then a metal crash from the street.*
Vini lies flat on the roof for an hour while the street below fills up, stares at nothing, and slowly empties again.
Rule eight: always boil or filter water before you drink it. Rain that runs over a dirty roof picks up dirt, bird droppings, and worse. A rolling boil for one minute kills most of the germs that can make you seriously ill. Out here, a stomach bug could finish him faster than a bite.
*Tracker: WATER rain barrel. FOOD 2 WEEKS. BATTERIES 6.*

## Day 18: The long nights (5:55–7:05) [BG07 city at night – TO MAKE, BG04]
Day eighteen. The power has been off for over two weeks. At night, the city is completely black, apart from the fires.
Tonight, Vini lights one candle. Just one, so he can read the back of a tin. Mistake number three.
Because in a dark city, one candle in a window can be seen from streets away.
Rule nine: at night, no light and no noise. He works that one out at three in the morning.
*Black screen. KNOCK. Pause. KNOCK. We are back where the video began.*
Something knocks on the front door. Once. Twice. Vini is behind the sofa. He doesn't move. He doesn't breathe. His leg goes to sleep. He lets it.
After an hour, it shuffles away.
From then on, Vini covers every gap in the boards, cooks only in daylight, and whispers even when he's talking to himself. Which, by now, is often.

## Day 24: The risk (7:05–8:20) [BG04]
Day twenty-four. The food is almost gone. Vini has four tins left, and one of them has no label. It's either peaches or dog food. He's saving it for a special occasion.
He switches on the battery radio, the one he grabbed back on day three. For three weeks, it's been nothing but static. Tonight, a voice.
"This is an emergency broadcast. An evacuation point is operating at the edge of the city. Evacuations end on day thirty."
Six days. Eleven kilometres. Through streets full of them.
Rule ten: when you finally move, travel light and travel in daylight. Water, food, torch, first aid kit. Nothing that rattles. Vini wraps his last tins in socks, so they won't clink. He has learned about tins.
*Tracker: FOOD 4 TINS. BATTERIES 2.*

## Day 30: The checkpoint (8:20–9:40) [BG03, BG08 checkpoint – TO MAKE]
Day thirty. Vini has been walking for two days, moving from car to car, hiding in gardens, freezing every time he hears a groan. Thirty days of doing almost everything right.
Then he sees it. Floodlights. Fences. Trucks. The checkpoint. He's two hundred metres away when his foot hits a can. It skids across the road, loud enough to echo off every house on the street.
Every head on the street turns towards him.
Vini runs. He runs faster than he has ever run in his life. The gate is closing. The groans are right behind him. He dives through the gap, and the gate slams shut.
*DAY 30 turns green. Stamp: SURVIVED.*
Thirty days. Vini survived. Mostly because of a bath full of water, a radio, and a lot of keeping quiet.
That night, in a tent, he finally opens the tin with no label. Special occasion.
*He looks inside. Long pause.*
It's dog food. He eats it anyway.

## The 10 rules
1. On day one, stay inside. 2. Fill everything with water. 3. Move at the quietest time and plan your route. 4. Grab food that lasts, plus batteries, torch, first aid, radio. 5. Never block your only way out. 6. Height is safety. 7. When the taps stop, collect rain. 8. Always boil or filter water. 9. At night, no light and no noise. 10. Travel light and in daylight.
