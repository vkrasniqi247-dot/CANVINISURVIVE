## Hook (0:00–0:30) [BG03 street]
This is Vini. Vini has never survived anything harder than a Monday. So when the news says the dead are walking through his city, he has one question. Can he survive thirty days?
There's one thing you need to know before we start. They can't see very well. But they can hear everything. So whatever happens… don't make a sound.
*Title card: SURVIVING 30 DAYS.*

## Day 1: Lock the door (0:30–1:50) [BG01 flat]
Day one. Vini wakes up to forty-seven missed calls and his phone screaming an emergency alert. Outside, someone is shouting. Then they stop shouting.
His first instinct is to run outside and see what's going on. That's what most people do. And that's why most people don't make it past day one.
Rule one: on day one, stay inside. Lock the door, close the curtains, and stay away from the windows. In the first hours of any disaster, the streets are the most dangerous place to be. Crashed cars, panicked crowds, and in this case, crowds that bite.
Rule two: fill everything with water. The bath, the sink, every pan and bottle he owns. When a city breaks down, the taps are often one of the first things to go. A person needs at least two litres of drinking water a day, and a full bath holds well over a hundred litres. That's weeks of water, sitting right there in the bathroom.
*Tracker: WATER full. FOOD 3 DAYS. BATTERIES 0.*
By midnight, the shouting outside has turned into something else. A low groan. Hundreds of them.

## Day 3: The supply run (1:50–3:30) [BG03 street, BG02 supermarket]
Day three. Vini's food is running out. He's eaten a family bag of crisps, half a jar of mayonnaise, and something from the back of the freezer he can't identify. He has to go out.
Rule three: move at the quietest time, and plan your route before you leave. The supermarket is four streets away. Four streets has never felt so far.
He creeps past abandoned cars, doors still open where people jumped out and ran. One car alarm, one dropped tin, and everything on this street will know exactly where he is.
Inside, most people grabbed the wrong things. Bread, fresh meat, milk. All of it goes off within days.
Rule four: grab food that lasts. Tins, rice, pasta, peanut butter, anything that doesn't need a fridge. Tinned food can stay safe to eat for years. And grab the things nobody thinks of: batteries, a torch, a first aid kit, and a battery radio.
Then he hears it. The trolley at the end of the aisle starts rolling. Slowly. On its own. Something pushed it.

## Day 7: The fortress (3:30–4:50) [BG04 barricaded flat, BG05 stairwell]
Day seven. Vini made it home. Just. Now he turns his flat into a fortress. He boards up the windows and pushes the bookcase against the door. But this is where most people get it wrong.
Rule five: never block your only way out. If they break in through the front, a sealed-up flat isn't a fortress. It's a trap. Vini makes sure he can still reach the stairwell, and from there, the roof.
Rule six: height is safety. Vini lives on the fifth floor, and the infected are terrible at stairs. He blocks the ground-floor stairwell door with furniture, so anything coming up has to make a lot of noise first. Noise he'll hear.
For the first time in a week, Vini sleeps.

## Day 12: The taps run dry (4:50–5:50) [BG06 rooftop]
Day twelve. The taps cough, splutter, and stop. The bath water is getting low.
Rule seven: when the taps stop, look up. Rain is the easiest clean water source in a British city, and Britain is not short of rain. Vini drags a tarp up to the roof and ties it between four poles, so the rain runs into buckets and a barrel underneath.
But rain that runs over a dirty roof picks up dirt, bird droppings, and worse.
Rule eight: always boil or filter water before you drink it. A rolling boil for one minute kills most of the germs that can make you seriously ill. And Vini can't afford to get ill. Out here, a stomach bug could finish him faster than a bite.

## Day 18: The long nights (5:50–7:00) [BG07 city at night – TO MAKE]
Day eighteen. The power has been off for over two weeks. At night, the city is completely black, apart from the fires. And this is when Vini learns the scariest lesson of all. In a dark city, one candle in a window can be seen from streets away.
Rule nine: at night, no light and no noise. Vini covers every gap in the boards, cooks only in daylight, and whispers even when he's talking to himself. Which, by now, is often.
*Black screen. KNOCK. Pause. KNOCK.*
At three in the morning, something knocks on the front door. Once. Twice. Vini doesn't move. He doesn't breathe. After an hour, it shuffles away.
Don't make a sound.

## Day 24: The risk (7:00–8:20) [BG04]
Day twenty-four. The food is almost gone. Vini has four tins left, and one of them has no label. It's either peaches or dog food. He's saving it for a special occasion.
He switches on the battery radio, the one he grabbed back on day three. For three weeks, it's been nothing but static. Tonight, a voice.
"This is an emergency broadcast. An evacuation point is operating at the edge of the city. Evacuations end on day thirty."
Six days. Eleven kilometres. Through streets full of them.
Rule ten: when you finally move, travel light and travel in daylight. Water, food, torch, first aid kit. Nothing that rattles. Vini wraps his last tins in socks, so they won't clink.

## Day 30: The checkpoint (8:20–9:40) [BG03, BG08 checkpoint – TO MAKE]
Day thirty. Vini has been walking for two days, moving from car to car, hiding in gardens, freezing every time he hears a groan.
Then he sees it. Floodlights. Fences. Trucks. The checkpoint. He's two hundred metres away when his foot hits a can. It skids across the road, loud enough to echo off every house on the street.
Every head on the street turns towards him.
Vini runs. He runs faster than he has ever run in his life. The gate is closing. The groans are right behind him. He dives through the gap, and the gate slams shut.
*DAY 30 turns green. Stamp: SURVIVED.*
Thirty days. Vini survived. Mostly because of a bath full of water, a radio, and a lot of keeping quiet.

## Outro (9:40–10:00)
So, would you have made it to day thirty? Comment the day you think you wouldn't have made it past. And if you want to see what Vini survives next, subscribe. Quietly.

## The 10 rules
1. On day one, stay inside. 2. Fill everything with water. 3. Move at the quietest time and plan your route. 4. Grab food that lasts, plus batteries, torch, first aid, radio. 5. Never block your only way out. 6. Height is safety. 7. When the taps stop, collect rain. 8. Always boil or filter water. 9. At night, no light and no noise. 10. Travel light and in daylight.
